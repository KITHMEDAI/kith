## Judge issues

**TS3003c**
- Minutes state Speaker D noted pricing for 'wood-finish covers and advanced chipset' needs to be obtained from manufacturing; the transcript only mentions Speaker D lacking pricing info on voice recognition ('I don't have any information on pricing, so I'll have to ask the manufacturing department') — the action item is written as 'Check manufacturing department for pricing information on voice recognition components' which is correct, but the follow-up section conflates this into chipset pricing, which is not in the transcript.
- Minutes describe Speaker B's market research slide as showing 'the target group would not pay more for voice recognition' — the transcript says the question was 'would you prefer it' not 'would you pay more for it'; Speaker B explicitly clarifies this distinction ('that doesn't really mean they wouldn't pay extra for it'), so characterising the slide as showing unwillingness to pay more slightly misrepresents the transcript.
- Follow-up note reads 'Whether the percentage of respondents who preferred the LCD screen (noted as higher than for voice recognition) affects the decision' — the transcript does not clearly state a percentage figure for LCD screen preference; describing it as 'a higher percentage' is a minor extrapolation beyond what is clearly established.

**LONG: 4 meetings stitched**
- The minutes state the Friday follow-up meeting is 'Friday (23 Oct)' but the note date is Thursday 22 October 2026, making Friday 23 October 2026 correct in principle — however, the transcript never provides a date or year, so the bracketed '(23 Oct)' date is derived purely from the note date metadata, not from a spoken day+date pair in the transcript. The rules state 'a year or date with no spoken day behind it counts as invented.' The day 'Friday' is spoken, but '23 Oct' is never spoken, making the bracketed date an invention.
- Similarly, 'Monday (26 Oct)' appears in action items. 'Monday' is spoken in the transcript, but '26 Oct' is never spoken, so the bracketed date is invented.
- The minutes state the progress report is 'noted as 23 February' — the transcript says 'June 23 February' (a speech-to-text error, but the closest reading is 'June 23' or 'February'), yet the minutes confidently render this as '23 February' without flagging the ambiguity as anything other than a missing year, when 'June' was also a possible transcription artifact.

**LONGEST: 6 meetings stitched**
- The minutes state the progress report date is 'June twenty third February' transcribed as '23 February' — Speaker D says 'The progress report is June twenty third February. Of February.' The transcript is garbled but the minutes interpret it as a clean '23 February' date and treat this as a firm deadline, when the transcript is actually unintelligible on this point; presenting it as a confirmed date adds false precision.
- The minutes state the profit aim is '€50,000,000' and then parenthetically note '(Speaker G queried whether this is for the first year; Sarah said she presumed so)' — the transcript has Sarah saying 'I presume so' which the minutes faithfully note, but the minutes also present the €50,000,000 figure as stated by Sarah when it appears on a slide shown during the meeting, not spoken by Sarah; minor but the attribution is imprecise.
- The minutes state under 'Decisions' for the remote control: 'Target group for the remote control is the 60–80 year old age group' — this is stated as a decision for all remote control meetings, but in the earlier kickoff/Sarah meeting the target group was described as 'everyone/all age groups'; the 60–80 target appears only in the later conceptual design meeting chaired by Speaker F. Presenting it as a single unified decision across all remote control meetings is misleading.
