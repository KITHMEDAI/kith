Progress note examples for depression are worth studying because depression notes drift toward sameness. Week after week the chart reads "patient reports low mood, discussed coping," and six months later nobody — including you — can tell whether anything changed. Below is one fictional session written three ways, in SOAP, DAP, and BIRP, so you can compare how each format handles the same material. After that, there's a short guide to what's worth capturing in depression-focused notes specifically, and the mistakes that make them less useful.

All three examples describe the same invented, generic vignette. They're not drawn from a real case, and they don't replace your own clinical judgment or your setting's documentation and risk policies.

## The session behind the examples

A patient in their late forties is in their fourth session, seen weekly for low mood that began after a redundancy several months ago. Last week you agreed on a small behavioural activation task: a 15-minute walk on three days. This week they managed it on two days, describe mood as "a bit flat but not as heavy," and report waking around 4am most nights. They've stopped meeting a friend they used to see weekly. In session you review the activity record, plan two more activities including a message to that friend, and spend some time on the thought "there's no point applying for anything." Risk is reviewed: passive thoughts that "things would be easier if I didn't wake up" earlier in the week, no plan or intent, and protective factors discussed.

## Depression progress note example: SOAP

```
SOAP Note — 27 October 2026 — Session #4

SUBJECTIVE
- Describes mood as "a bit flat but not as heavy" compared with
  last week
- Early-morning waking (~4am) on most nights; appetite reduced but
  eating regular meals
- Completed planned 15-minute walk on 2 of 3 agreed days; said the
  second walk "felt slightly better than expected"
- Has not seen a close friend in about six weeks
- Reports passive thoughts earlier this week ("easier if I didn't
  wake up"); denies plan, intent, or preparatory behaviour
- PHQ-9 today: [score] (previous: [score])

OBJECTIVE
- Casually dressed, appropriate hygiene
- Slowed speech, limited eye contact early in session; more
  animated when discussing the walks
- Affect constricted, congruent with reported mood
- Engaged and brought activity record

ASSESSMENT
- Depressive symptoms persist, with early signs of response to
  behavioural activation (partial task completion, some reported
  mood lift linked to activity)
- Social withdrawal is a maintaining factor
- Passive ideation without plan or intent; protective factors
  include family and engagement in therapy. Risk reviewed per
  service policy

PLAN
- Continue walks, 3 days this week
- Add one social activity: message friend to arrange a coffee
- Continue work on "no point applying" thought next session
- Re-review risk at start of next session; safety plan
  information provided
```

SOAP's separation of what the patient tells you from what you observe is especially useful in depression. Self-report often lags behind visible change — or runs ahead of it — and having slowed speech or a brightening affect recorded in Objective gives you a second data stream when you look back. The blank format is in our [SOAP note template](/blog/soap-note-templates) post.

Note the placeholders for the PHQ-9. If you use a standardised measure, record the actual score; we've left it blank here rather than invent numbers.

## Depression progress note example: DAP

```
DAP Note — 27 October 2026 — Session #4

DATA
- Mood "a bit flat but not as heavy"; early-morning waking most
  nights; appetite reduced, regular meals
- Walks completed 2 of 3 agreed days; reported slight lift after
  the second
- No contact with close friend for ~6 weeks
- Passive thoughts earlier in the week; denies plan, intent, or
  preparatory behaviour
- Slowed speech and limited eye contact early, more animated when
  discussing activity; constricted affect
- Session: reviewed activity record, scheduled next week's
  activities, began examining "there's no point applying for
  anything"

ASSESSMENT
- Early partial response to behavioural activation
- Withdrawal from friendship maintaining low mood
- Hopelessness about job search is a key cognitive target
- Passive ideation, no plan or intent; risk reviewed per service
  policy

PLAN
- Walks 3 days; message friend to arrange coffee
- Continue cognitive work on job-search hopelessness
- Re-review risk next session
```

DAP is quicker to write because reported and observed material share one section. For depression, the cost is that it's easier to blur "patient says they're fine" with "patient looks fine." If you use DAP, it helps to keep observations on their own lines within Data, as above. More on the format in our [DAP note template](/blog/dap-note-templates) post.

## Depression progress note example: BIRP

```
BIRP Note — 27 October 2026 — Session #4

BEHAVIOR
- Mood "a bit flat but not as heavy"; early-morning waking most
  nights
- Walks 2 of 3 agreed days; no contact with close friend ~6 weeks
- Slowed speech, limited eye contact early in session
- Passive thoughts earlier in the week; denies plan, intent, or
  preparatory behaviour

INTERVENTION
- Reviewed activity record and linked activity to mood ratings
- Collaboratively scheduled next week's activities, including one
  social contact
- Began examining the thought "there's no point applying for
  anything"
- Risk review; discussed protective factors and safety plan
  information

RESPONSE
- Noticed the walk days were the "less heavy" days without
  prompting
- Hesitant but agreed to message friend; identified a specific day
- Could name one counter-example to the "no point" thought;
  belief in it still high
- Engaged with risk discussion; identified family as a reason
  for living

PLAN
- Walks 3 days; message friend
- Continue cognitive work next session
- Re-review risk at start of next session
```

BIRP's strength is the explicit pairing of intervention and response. In depression work that pairing is often the most useful thing in the chart: noticing the link between activity and mood *for themselves* is a different clinical event from being told about it. ## What to document in depression progress notes

A few things make depression notes more useful on review, whichever format you choose.

**Mood in the patient's words, plus your observation.** "Low mood" is a label. "A bit flat but not as heavy," alongside slowed speech and constricted affect, is a picture you can compare next month.

**Neurovegetative symptoms.** Sleep, appetite, energy, and concentration often shift before self-reported mood does. A brief line on each relevant one gives the chart an early-warning signal.

**Activity and withdrawal.** What the patient actually *did* this week — tasks completed, contact avoided — is often the clearest marker of change in depression, and it's central if you're using behavioural activation.

**Standardised measures, with dates.** If your practice uses the PHQ-9 or a similar measure, put the score in the note itself so the trend sits in the session record, not in a separate spreadsheet.

**Risk, every time it's relevant.** Depression notes are where risk documentation matters most. Record what you asked, what the patient said, protective factors, and what you did. Follow your own service's policy and frameworks — a progress note example is not a risk assessment tool.

**The specific intervention.** "Discussed coping strategies" could describe any session. "Scheduled two activities including one social contact" tells the next reader exactly where the work is.

## Common mistakes in depression progress notes

**Copying last week's note forward.** Depression can look similar session to session, which makes copy-paste tempting. It also hides the slow, small changes that are often the whole story of a depression treatment.

**Recording risk only when it's present.** If you asked and the patient denied, write that down. An absent risk line is ambiguous; a brief "denies" line isn't.

**Only recording deficits.** Notes that list symptoms and never the walk that happened, or the counter-example the patient found, give a distorted picture of the treatment.

**Vague plans.** "Continue BA" isn't a plan. "Walks three days, message friend to arrange coffee" is — and it's what you'll check at the start of the next session.
