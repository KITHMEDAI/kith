If you're a therapist in India searching for practice management software, most of what comes up first is built for someone else's market. The big global names are built around US insurance billing and USD pricing, and a lot of "best of 2026" roundups are written from that same US-first vantage point with India mentioned as an afterthought, if at all. This is a more grounded look at what's actually available to a solo or small-practice therapist in India right now — what a handful of India-built and India-available tools actually charge, what they actually do, and where each one fits (or doesn't) depending on how your practice runs day to day.

## What actually matters when choosing practice management software in India

Before the comparisons, it's worth naming the things that quietly decide whether a tool works for an Indian practice, because most "best practice management software" lists don't ask about them:

- **How clients actually pay.** UPI is the default for most Indian clients, not cards. A booking flow that only takes card payments is friction most of your clients won't push through.
- **Where reminders land.** WhatsApp is where Indian clients actually check messages; a tool built around US-style SMS or email reminders often gets ignored.
- **Currency and billing.** INR pricing with a local payment method beats a USD subscription that adds currency conversion and international transaction fees on top of the sticker price.
- **Compliance framing.** India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is the law that actually governs a therapy practice here — we've written a [full breakdown of what DPDP requires](/blog/dpdp-2023-therapist-notes). A tool that only talks about HIPAA compliance is describing a different country's law, not necessarily failing to protect your data, but not built with DPDP's specific consent and cross-border transfer rules in mind either.
- **Whether it's actually a fit for therapy**, not a generic clinic scheduler adapted for any small business.

With that in mind, here's how five options that show up in this search actually stack up.

## Carepatron

Carepatron is one of the more prominent global players that gets recommended broadly, including to Indian practices, mostly on the strength of its free plan. That free tier is genuinely generous: unlimited clients, telehealth, 1GB of storage, a client portal, automated online payments, and a built-in AI scribe, all at $0/month. Paid tiers (Plus and Advanced) add unlimited storage, group telehealth, custom branding, and role-based permissions, currently discounted to $15.50 and $19.50 per user per month respectively for the first six months (regular price $31 and $39).

For India specifically, the gap is straightforward: everything is priced and billed in USD, and there's no UPI payment option or India-specific billing built in. Clients would need to pay by card, and you'd be managing currency conversion on every subscription payment. It's a strong general-purpose tool with a real free plan, but it wasn't built with an Indian client base's payment habits in mind.

## SimplePractice

SimplePractice is the name most likely to come up if you search generically for practice management software, because it dominates that search in the US. It's a genuinely full platform: scheduling with client self-booking, integrated telehealth, customizable notes and intake forms, a client portal, and insurance claims filing. Pricing runs Starter at $49/month, Essential at $79/month, and Plus at $99/month, with an AI note-taking add-on priced separately on top of any tier.

The insurance claims workflow — arguably the biggest reason US therapists choose it — does nothing for a therapist billing Indian clients directly. Account access and support are also built around US business hours and USD billing, and the compliance framing is HIPAA, not DPDP. We've covered this comparison in more depth in [Kith vs SimplePractice](/blog/kith-vs-simplepractice) if you want the full picture, but the short version for this list: it's a capable platform that wasn't built for an Indian practice's payment methods, currency, or compliance law, and you'd be paying a US-tier price for features (insurance billing) you likely won't use here.

## ProTherapy

ProTherapy is one of a small number of tools built specifically for Indian therapists rather than adapted afterward. It combines a shareable booking page, pre-session intake, and payment into one flow, with UPI, cash, bank transfer, and Razorpay card payments all supported and zero platform commission on session fees. Session notes are encrypted, stored on servers within India, and organized with reusable templates. Reminders go out over WhatsApp — 24 hours and 1 hour before a session — along with booking confirmations and payment nudges, which matters more for reducing no-shows than most tools' email reminders do in practice. It also includes a client-facing app with homework, resources, and an optional mood check-in between sessions.

Pricing is ₹499/month for the first three months, then ₹999/month, or ₹6,990/year (about ₹583/month), with a 7-day free trial and no card required to start. That's a meaningfully lower price point than the global platforms above, in a currency and with payment rails that match how Indian clients actually pay. What it doesn't do is AI-generated clinical notes — its documentation side is encrypted templates you fill in yourself, not a transcript-based note pipeline.

## PractiPal

PractiPal is another India-built option, aimed specifically at mental health practitioners, psychologists, and counselors. It covers Google Calendar sync with automated confirmations and WhatsApp reminders, invoice generation with payment tracking and a financial dashboard, encrypted and searchable session notes, a client portal for sharing worksheets, and basic practice analytics like revenue and client-retention tracking.

Pricing is in INR with a genuine free tier (Graduate Journey: up to 5 clients, 1GB storage, UPI payments included), then Growing Ensemble at ₹1,499/month for unlimited clients and 5GB storage, and Therapy Empire at ₹1,899/month for multi-therapist practices, each with a 14-day free trial. Like ProTherapy, notes here are structured templates you write into yourself rather than AI-generated from a session — the free tier is a genuine option worth trying before you commit to either paid tier or a different tool entirely.

## Kith

Kith takes a different approach on the clinical documentation side specifically: rather than a template you fill in, it listens to the session — through your device mic for in-person sessions, or through a notetaker bot that joins the call for online sessions on Google Meet — and turns the transcript into a structured note through a two-layer AI pipeline (a compression pass, then a structuring pass), producing a SOAP note, key points, a session summary, homework, and a next-session plan. Recording consent and AI-processing consent are tracked as two separate, server-side gates rather than one bundled checkbox. On the practice-management side, Kith offers conflict-checked scheduling, recurring appointments, an auto-created Google Meet link for each online booking, patient records imported from an existing CSV or Excel file, and PDF export of any session note. Sensitive fields are encrypted at rest with per-therapist row-level security, aligned to DPDP specifically rather than retrofitted from HIPAA.

To be direct about what's not here yet: Kith's calendar sync with Google Calendar is one-way (it pulls events in; it doesn't push changes back out, aside from creating the Meet link for a Kith-made booking), reminders are an in-app toast rather than automated WhatsApp or SMS messages to you, and there's no UPI or in-app payment collection from clients at all — Kith doesn't handle billing or invoicing today. Kith's paid tiers also aren't live yet, so the free plan is currently the only plan, with no card required to start.

If your priority is automatic, ambient AI notes from sessions you're already having, Kith does that more directly than any of the other four tools here, none of which generate notes from the session itself. If your priority is UPI-based client billing or WhatsApp reminders sent to clients, ProTherapy or PractiPal currently cover that ground and Kith doesn't.

## Which one actually fits your practice

If client payments and WhatsApp reminders are the friction points in your practice right now, ProTherapy or PractiPal are the more complete answer today — both are built around UPI and WhatsApp from the ground up, and both have been live in the Indian market longer with that specific workflow. If you want a broad, mature, full-featured platform and don't mind paying in USD and working around a compliance framework built for the US, SimplePractice or Carepatron are real options, with Carepatron's free tier being the more generous starting point of the two. If what's actually eating your time is writing up notes after every session, and you'd rather that draft itself from the conversation than from a template, Kith is the option built specifically around that, free to start, with no billing or reminder features to configure because it doesn't try to be a full practice-management suite yet.

None of these five is a complete answer to every part of running a practice in India today — the honest takeaway is that the India-specific tools currently lead on payments and reminders, while Kith currently leads on how the clinical note itself gets made, and picking between them means picking which part of the admin burden you want solved first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a genuinely free practice management tool for therapists in India?**
Yes — Kith, Carepatron, and PractiPal all have real free tiers (not just trials) as of this writing, though each caps or narrows what's included differently: Kith's free plan is currently its only plan, Carepatron's free tier is broad but USD-billed, and PractiPal's free tier caps at 5 clients.

**Which of these tools supports UPI payments from clients?**
ProTherapy and PractiPal both support UPI directly, since they're built specifically for the Indian market. Kith, Carepatron, and SimplePractice do not currently take UPI payments.

**Do any of these tools generate session notes automatically from the conversation?**
Kith and Carepatron both offer AI-generated notes from a session; ProTherapy and PractiPal provide encrypted templates you write into yourself rather than AI-generated notes; SimplePractice's AI note-taking is a separate paid add-on rather than a built-in feature.

**Is DPDP compliance the same as HIPAA compliance?**
No. They're different laws with different consent, data-residency, and breach-notification requirements. A tool marketed as HIPAA-compliant hasn't necessarily addressed DPDP's specific rules, and vice versa — worth checking directly with any vendor rather than assuming one compliance claim covers the other.
