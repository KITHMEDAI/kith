## Twitter/X

Kith pulls your last 4 sessions - summary, homework, next-session plan, risk level - when drafting a new note. It tracks trajectory across sessions instead of just today vs. last time, and flags it if something planned two sessions ago never got followed up on.

## Instagram

Most note tools compare a session to the one right before it, if they compare at all. That's fine for a single check-in, but it misses the pattern that actually matters with longer-term clients - whether the thing you planned to focus on three sessions ago ever actually got addressed.

When Kith drafts a new note, it pulls the last 4 completed sessions - summary, homework assigned, next-session plan, risk level - and uses that to ground the new note in trajectory, not just a single comparison point. If a planned focus or piece of homework quietly fell off the radar a couple sessions back, it gets flagged instead of silently resetting.

It's not treatment-plan writing - it's continuity tracking. For caseloads that lean long-term or complex, that's usually the harder problem.
