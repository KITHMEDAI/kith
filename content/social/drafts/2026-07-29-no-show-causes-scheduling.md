## Twitter/X

Not every "no-show" is the patient forgetting. Some are a double-booked slot, or a Meet link that was hard to find. That's a calendar problem, not a reminder problem - worth telling the two apart before you try to fix either one.

## Instagram

When a session gets missed, it's tempting to file it under "no-show" and move on. But not all missed sessions have the same cause, and lumping them together means you end up fixing the wrong thing.

Some are genuine forgetting. Some are ambivalence about the work itself - which is a clinical conversation, not a scheduling one. And some, more often than people admit, are logistics: a double-booked slot, a recurring appointment that quietly dropped, or an online session where the video link was nowhere to be found five minutes before start time.

That last category is the one software can actually close. Kith checks new bookings against your existing schedule before confirming them, and creates the Google Meet link automatically the moment an online session is booked - so there's no separate step where a link can go missing.

It won't fix ambivalence, and it won't replace a direct conversation about a pattern of missed sessions. But it does mean fewer of your no-shows are the avoidable kind.
