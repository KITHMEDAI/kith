## Twitter/X

The easiest way to spot a note that isn't doing its job: "continue treatment as before." That's not a plan - it's the absence of one. kith.space/blog/soap-note-templates

## Instagram

"Continue treatment as before" shows up in a lot of Plan sections, and it's worth noticing why it's a problem: it's not actually a plan. It's what you write when nothing's been decided yet.

A Plan section only earns its place in the note if it says something specific - what changes next session, what the patient's doing between now and then, whether the approach is shifting at all. If none of that changed, that itself is worth a sentence, not a placeholder phrase.

Same goes for Objective sections that describe things nobody actually observed that day, or Subjective notes that quietly drift into your own interpretation instead of the patient's words. Small habits, but they're the difference between a chart that tells the story of someone's treatment and one that just fills a requirement.
