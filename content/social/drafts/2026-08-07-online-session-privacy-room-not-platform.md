## Twitter/X

The biggest confidentiality risk in an online session usually isn't the video platform - it's who else is in the room. Headphones, a closed door, and a backup phone number agreed on in advance do more than any encryption checkbox. Easy to skip, easy to fix.

## Instagram

We talk a lot about encryption and consent forms when it comes to online therapy privacy - and those matter. But the most common privacy risk we actually hear about from therapists running sessions on Google Meet or Zoom isn't technical at all.

It's whether the client (and you) are actually alone in the room. A partner walking past. A roommate on the other side of a thin door. A laptop mic picking up more of the house than intended.

Headphones, a closed door, and a backup phone number agreed on in advance cost nothing and catch most of what actually goes wrong mid-session - long before you get to which platform's encryption is technically stronger.

Kith's notetaker bot joins the call quietly to handle the session notes, but that's the easy technical part. The privacy risk worth actually managing is the one in the room, not the one in the software.
